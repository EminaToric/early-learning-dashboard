Why I Built This
I have a Master of Science in Child and Human Development, which means I know what happens in a child's brain during the first five years. 90% of brain architecture is built before age 5. The connections formed during this period — through play, language, relationships, and safe environments — are the foundation everything else is built on.
Most people know early childhood education matters. This project puts the data behind that claim and shows exactly where the gaps are, who is bearing the cost of them, and what the numbers actually mean for real children.

Key Findings

In Sub-Saharan Africa, fewer than one in three children is enrolled in any pre-primary program. In Europe it is more than four in five. Same age, completely different starting line.
Latin America spends more on pre-primary education as a share of GDP than any other region — including wealthier regions. That is a policy choice, not an accident.
Sub-Saharan Africa has nearly 28 children per teacher. Once you understand child development you know what that number means for language, self-regulation, and school readiness.
Where gender gaps in access exist, they compound. Missing pre-primary makes a girl less likely to start primary on time and more likely to leave school early.
